# Maths in real life: practice units: teacher's key with CFI diagnosis

*PISA-style practice items written from original templates that follow the published PISA 2022 mathematics framework. They are not official PISA items and have not been field-tested. Numbers in tables are made up for practice.*

Every wrong option, wrong true/false judgement and common wrong written answer below is labelled with a Confusion Fingerprint Index category: RF (Recall Failure), PK (Partial Knowledge), CF (Confabulation, a confident misconception), INT (Interference, the right answer to a different question). Use the labels to decide what to reteach, not just who got it wrong.

**Answer: D** (200 + 20x)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 20 + 200x | CF, Confabulation: swapped the fixed charge and the rate per GB |

**No credit:** Other responses, or no response.

| Common wrong answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Plan B, because it has no monthly charge. | CF, Confabulation: believes a plan with no fixed fee is always cheaper, without checking the cost at this amount of data |
| Works out both plans for 4 GB, the amount in Question 1. | INT, Interference: answered for a different amount of data, not the 18 GB asked about |
| Works out the cost of one plan only and chooses it. | PK, Partial Knowledge: started the comparison but did not finish it |

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |

## Unit 3: Fencing the school garden

*Space and shape, Occupational context. Unit ID PISA_SCHOOL_GARDEN_0003.*

### Question 3.1 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: D** (38 m)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 18 m | CF, Confabulation: believes the distance around a rectangle is just length plus width |
| B | 40 m | PK, Partial Knowledge: worked out the full distance around but forgot to leave out the gate |
| C | 96 m | INT, Interference: found the area of the garden instead of the distance around it |

### Question 3.2 (Mathematical reasoning, multiple choice)

**Answer: A** (It is 4 times as large.)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| B | It is the same, because it is the same shape. | RF, Recall Failure: no working link between the sides and the area |
| C | It is 2 times as large. | CF, Confabulation: assumes area grows in the same proportion as the sides, a well-known misconception about scaling |
| D | It is 8 times as large. | INT, Interference: used the rule for scaling a volume, right for a different kind of quantity |

### Question 3.3 (Interpret and evaluate, constructed response)

**Full credit:** No. The fencing costs 50 × 38 = Rs 1,900, which is more than Rs 1,700.

**Partial credit:** Works out a cost with one slip (for example forgets the gate) and gives the conclusion that matches that cost.

**No credit:** Other responses, or no response.

| Common wrong answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Multiplies Rs 50 by the area (96) and gets Rs 4,800. | INT, Interference: used the area instead of the fence length, the area and perimeter mix-up again |
| Finds the cost of one side only, Rs 600. | PK, Partial Knowledge: started the calculation but did not cover the whole fence |
| Says yes or no with no calculation. | RF, Recall Failure: no link between the budget and the cost |

## Unit 4: Bus to the district headquarters

*Quantity, Societal context. Unit ID PISA_BUS_JOURNEY_0004.*

### Question 4.1 (Formulate, multiple choice)

**Answer: C** (100 ÷ 40)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 40 ÷ 100 | CF, Confabulation: turned the division upside down |
| B | 100 − 40 | RF, Recall Failure: combined the two numbers with no link to how speed works |
| D | 100 × 40 | INT, Interference: used distance = speed × time, which answers a different question |

### Question 4.2 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: B** (1:55 pm)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1:45 pm | PK, Partial Knowledge: forgot to add the 10-minute stop |
| C | 1:05 pm | RF, Recall Failure: used the distance in kilometres as if it were minutes |
| D | 2:15 pm | CF, Confabulation: read 2.5 hours as 2 hours 50 minutes, treating the decimal part as minutes |

### Question 4.3 (Interpret and evaluate, constructed response)

**Full credit:** No. The whole trip takes 160 minutes for 100 km, so the average for the whole trip is about 37.5 km/h, less than 40 km/h, because the bus covers no distance during the stop. An explanation without the number also gets full credit.

**No credit:** Other responses, or no response.

| Common wrong answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Yes, because the bus drives at 40 km/h. | PK, Partial Knowledge: knows what speed means but forgot that the stop adds time without adding distance |
| No, the average is 20 km/h, halfway between 40 and 0. | CF, Confabulation: averaged the two speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time |
| Works out 100 ÷ 40 again and says yes. | INT, Interference: repeated the driving-time calculation, which answers a different question |

## Unit 5: The price of dal

*Quantity, Personal context. Unit ID PISA_DAL_PRICE_0005.*

### Question 5.1 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: B** (Rs 240)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | Rs 40 | PK, Partial Knowledge: found the increase but did not add it to the old price |
| C | Rs 220 | CF, Confabulation: added the percentage as if it were rupees |
| D | Rs 160 | INT, Interference: applied a decrease instead of an increase |

### Question 5.2 (Mathematical reasoning, multiple choice)

**Answer: A** (It is Rs 192, less than the original Rs 200.)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| B | It is Rs 200, the same as the original price. | CF, Confabulation: believes a rise and a fall of the same percentage cancel out, a common and confident misconception |
| C | It is Rs 288, even higher than before. | INT, Interference: applied another rise instead of a fall |
| D | It is Rs 180. | RF, Recall Failure: subtracted the percentage from the original price with no clear reason |

### Question 5.3 (Interpret and evaluate, constructed response)

**Full credit:** 3 kg. 891 ÷ 240 = 3.71, and he cannot pay for another whole kilogram.

**No credit:** Other responses, or no response.

| Common wrong answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| 4 kg | CF, Confabulation: rounded up as usual, but he does not have enough money for the last kilogram |
| 3.71 kg | PK, Partial Knowledge: divided correctly but did not turn the result into whole kilograms |
| 4 kg | INT, Interference: used the old price of Rs 200 |

## Unit 6: How we get to school

*Uncertainty and data, Societal context. Unit ID PISA_TRAVEL_SURVEY_0006.*

### Question 6.1 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: C** (28%)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 42.4% | CF, Confabulation: divided by the wrong total, only walkers and cyclists, instead of the whole class |
| B | 38% | INT, Interference: worked out the percentage for walkers, a different group |
| D | 14% | PK, Partial Knowledge: used the number of cyclists as if it were a percentage |

### Question 6.2 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: B** (13/50)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1/4 | CF, Confabulation: assumed the four ways of travelling are equally likely, ignoring the counts |
| C | 13/37 | INT, Interference: compared bus users with everyone else (odds) instead of with the whole class |
| D | 37/50 | PK, Partial Knowledge: found the right total but counted the students who do not take the bus |

### Question 6.3 (Mathematical reasoning, constructed response)

**Full credit:** Either reason: fewer than half of this class walk (19 of 50), so 'most' is not true even for this class; or one class may not represent the whole school.

**No credit:** Other responses, or no response.

| Common wrong answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| She is right, because walking has the biggest number (19). | CF, Confabulation: believes the biggest group must be a majority, more than half |
| She is right, because many students like walking. | RF, Recall Failure: an opinion with no link to the data |
| She is wrong, because only 14 students cycle. | INT, Interference: used a row of the table that does not bear on the claim |

## Unit 7: The rooftop water tank

*Space and shape, Occupational context. Unit ID PISA_WATER_TANK_0007.*

### Question 7.1 (Formulate, multiple choice)

**Answer: B** (1 × 2 × 1.5 × 1000)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1 × 2 × 1.5 × 100 | CF, Confabulation: used 1 cubic metre = 100 litres, a wrong conversion factor |
| C | 1 × 2 × 1.5 | PK, Partial Knowledge: found the volume in cubic metres but did not convert it to litres |
| D | 2 × (1 × 2 + 2 × 1.5 + 1 × 1.5) × 1000 | INT, Interference: used the area of the tank's faces instead of its volume |

### Question 7.2 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: D** (150 minutes)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 20 minutes | RF, Recall Failure: repeated a number from the question with no calculation |
| B | 2.5 minutes | PK, Partial Knowledge: changed the answer to hours but kept the word minutes |

**Full credit:** 8 days. Half the tank is 1,500 litres, and 1,500 ÷ 180 = 8.33, so the water lasts 8 full days.

**No credit:** Other responses, or no response.

| Common wrong answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| 16 days | INT, Interference: used the full tank, the answer to a different situation |
| 9 days | CF, Confabulation: rounded up, but the water runs out during the last day |
| 8.33 days | PK, Partial Knowledge: divided correctly but did not interpret the answer as whole days |

## Unit 8: Two kinds of growth

*Change and relationships, Scientific context, PISA 2022 emphasis on growth phenomena. Unit ID PISA_GROWTH_0008.*

### Question 8.1 (Employ, multiple choice)

**Answer: D** (16 thousand)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A | 110 thousand | INT, Interference: worked out the count for sample B instead of sample A |
| B | 8 thousand | PK, Partial Knowledge: stopped one doubling early |
| C | 12 thousand | CF, Confabulation: treated doubling as multiplying by 2 times the hours, linear thinking about growth |

### Question 8.2 (Mathematical reasoning, multiple choice)

**Answer: A** (After 7 hours)

| If a child picks | Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| B | After 5 hours | INT, Interference: compared sample A with sample B's starting size instead of its size at that hour |
| C | Never, because sample B starts bigger. | CF, Confabulation: believes the sample that starts bigger stays ahead, ignoring how doubling speeds up |
| D | After 6 hours | PK, Partial Knowledge: stopped one hour early, before sample A had overtaken |

### Question 8.3 (Interpret and evaluate, complex multiple choice)

| Statement | Answer | If a child gets it wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Sample A gains the same number of cells every hour. | False | CF, Confabulation: assumed all growth is linear |
| Sample B gains the same number of cells every hour. | True | PK, Partial Knowledge: did not connect 'gains 20 thousand cells every hour' with a constant amount |
| After 9 hours, sample A has more than twice as many cells as sample B. | True | RF, Recall Failure: guessed without working out the two counts |

Full credit for all statements right.
